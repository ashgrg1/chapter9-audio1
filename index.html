    The big idea

    Pharmacotherapy is treating disease with medicines. As a technician you should know, for common drugs: the brand ↔ generic name, category, dosage forms, common side effects, key interactions, and storage.

    Pharmacokinetics · what the body does to the drug
    AAbsorption
    Into the blood. IV skips it. Bioavailability = fraction that reaches blood.
    DDistribution
    Into tissues. Fat-loving drugs = large volume, stay long.
    MMetabolism
    Mostly liver, CYP 450 (e.g. 3A4). First-pass. Prodrugs activate.
    EExcretion
    Urine, feces, lungs. Half-life = time to drop 50%.

    Pharmacodynamics is the reverse: what the drug does to the body (dose ↔ effect).

    Building blocks

    • Atomic number = protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons.
    • 96% of the body: O, C, H, N.
    • Enzymes are proteins that speed reactions; 20 amino acids build proteins.
    • 46 chromosomes per cell (23 from each parent). Genes code proteins.

    11 organ systems

    • Integumentary, skeletal (206 bones), muscular, nervous, endocrine, cardiovascular, lymphatic, respiratory, digestive, urinary, reproductive.
    • Muscles: skeletal (voluntary), cardiac, smooth (involuntary).
    • Afferent → to brain. Efferent → from brain.
    • Pancreas: insulin ↓ glucose, glucagon ↑ glucose.

    Body systems & drugs

    • Stomach absorbs little (except alcohol); small intestine absorbs most.
    • Liver = main metabolizer; first-pass lowers bioavailability.
    • Kidneys excrete drugs, balance electrolytes, control BP.
    • Plasma minus clotting proteins = serum.

    Disease words

    • Homeostasis = balance. Pathophysiology = what goes wrong.
    • Epidemiology = studying populations (food-poisoning example).
    • Risk factors raise disease odds. Pharmacogenomics = genes guide dosing.
    • Treatments: lifestyle, cut risk factors, surgery, pharmacotherapy.

    Dosage forms

    • Say tablet or capsule, not "pill". Capsules are cheapest to make.
    • Don't crush ER / CR / DR tablets.
    • Solution = clear. Suspension = never clear, shake well.
    • Syrup = sweet. Elixir = contains alcohol.
    • Cream = water-based. Ointment = oil-based. Not interchangeable.
    • Injectable suspensions: IM only, never IV.

    Safety terms

    • Narrow therapeutic index: digoxin, warfarin. Shelve strengths apart.
    • Side effect = normal dose. Toxic effect = too much.
    • Contraindication = don't use. Precaution = use with care.
    • Cross-sensitivity: allergy to one penicillin → others.
    • Off-label = use not approved by FDA.
    • Inducer speeds an enzyme; inhibitor slows it (erythromycin ↑ carbamazepine).

    Watch on the exam

    • Signs vs symptoms: your textbook says signs = what the patient describes, symptoms = what the provider finds. Standard medical references reverse this. Use the textbook for your module exam; know the standard version for the PTCE.
    • Ranitidine (Zantac) is still in the textbook table, but it was pulled from the US market in 2020.
    • Tramadol is listed as "not an opiate," but it is a controlled substance (C-IV) that acts on opioid receptors.
    • Generic name must be at least half the size of the brand name on the label.
